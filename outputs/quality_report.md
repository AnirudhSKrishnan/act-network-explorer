## How each check works, and its caveats

**Graph model.** Every OSM way is an edge (split where another way T-joins it). Endpoints within `tol` are merged into one junction node. Substations and transformers are nodes of their own, linked to the endpoints that reach them. A component counts as *fed* only if it contains a substation; transformers connect LV to 11 kV but are not supply points.

**1. Floating components.** Connected pieces of network with no substation. `isolated` = no substation and no transformer; `transformer_only` = reaches a transformer but the 11 kV side behind it is not mapped or not joined. Pieces that cross the ACT boundary are excluded because they may be fed from NSW. *Caveat:* OSM rarely maps 11 kV underground cables leaving zone substations or the transformers between LV and 11 kV, so a floating LV or 11 kV section usually means *incomplete mapping*, not a mistake. Floating *transmission* sections are counted as likely errors.

**2. Dangling ends.** Original way ends that touch no other way (end-to-end or T-junction), no substation and no transformer. *Caveat:* a radial LV or 11 kV feeder legitimately ends at its last pole, so LV / 11 kV / untagged ends are listed as *unconnected ends to check*, not errors. Ends within 20 m of another way are marked `near_miss` (probable unjoined gap, the best candidates to fix). Transmission lines should end at a substation, so a transmission dangling end is a likely error (a missing substation or a gap).

**3. Missing voltage.** `power=line/minor_line/cable` ways whose `voltage` tag is missing or unparseable. A tag gap, not a topology error.

**4. Voltage conflicts.** A junction (no substation or transformer within the tolerances) where two ways with *disjoint* known voltage sets meet. Multi-valued tags count as all their voltages, so `11000;415` meeting `415` is fine (LV underbuilt on 11 kV poles), but `11000` meeting `415` is flagged. Untagged ways never conflict. Counted per junction. LV meeting 11/22 kV (`lv_vs_hv_distribution`) is almost always an LV way starting at a pole whose transformer is not mapped, i.e. missing equipment, so it is a *to check* item, not an error; other pairs (e.g. 22 kV meeting 11 kV, or transmission meeting distribution) are likely errors.

**5. Duplicates and overlaps.** Exact duplicates have identical geometry (either direction); one row per redundant copy. A substantial overlap is a way lying within 1 m of another for at least 10 m and at least 50% of its own length. Shorter partial overlaps, mostly separately mapped 132 kV circuits converging onto shared towers near substations, are counted but not reported. Duplicate substations: same name (likely error), or one footprint mostly inside another / a point inside a footprint (to check: may be a genuine site-within-a-site).

## Limitations

- OSM power data in the ACT is incomplete (many underground 11 kV cables, transformers and service lines are missing), so absence in OSM is not absence on the ground.
- Results depend on the tolerances above; see the sensitivity table.
- Lines that merely cross (no shared OSM node, no end near the other line) are *not* connected, because overhead lines cross without joining.
- Transformers include both `power=transformer` features and pole-top transformers mapped as a `transformer=*` tag on a `power=pole` node; OSM still maps only a small fraction of the real distribution transformers, so most LV-vs-11 kV conflicts are unmapped equipment.
- osmnx returns whole ways that intersect the ACT, so the whole-way totals include line length in NSW (mostly 330 kV and 66 kV); use the inside-the-ACT figures for the ACT network.
- Counts by type: dangling_end: 4358, duplicate_substation: 2, floating_component: 764, missing_voltage: 626, overlapping_lines: 26, voltage_conflict: 268.
